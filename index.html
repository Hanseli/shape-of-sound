THE SURVIVOR FILTER

Popularity is a signal from now,
not a chart from then.

Spotify's popularity score (0–100) describes a track's current standing on the platform. It is not a reconstruction of its historical chart rank. A high score for a song released decades ago is a trace of its present-day listening life: an old recording still circulating in a modern streaming catalog.

We call that a survivor. The Survivor Filter compares high-popularity older tracks with faded tracks from the same decade, then sets their audio fingerprints beside contemporary music. It can reveal patterns in this catalog—but it cannot tell us why a listener presses play.

A FIELD GUIDE TO THE DATA

What Spotify measures—and what it doesn’t

Spotify’s audio attributes are estimates, not direct measurements of a song’s cultural meaning. These definitions follow Spotify’s documentation; this project analyzes a static Kaggle dataset rather than making live API requests.

Sound & feeling

Acousticness 0–1
Confidence that a track is acoustic; higher means more likely acoustic.
Danceability 0–1
How suitable a track is for dancing, based on factors such as tempo, rhythm stability, beat strength, and regularity.
Energy 0–1
A perceptual measure of intensity and activity. Louder, faster, and more intense tracks tend to score higher.
Valence 0–1
Musical positivity: higher values tend to sound more positive; lower values more negative.
Loudness dB
Average overall loudness across a track, measured in decibels. Values are typically between −60 and 0 dB.

Voice & performance

Instrumentalness 0–1
How likely a track contains no vocals; values closer to 1 indicate stronger instrumental confidence.
Speechiness 0–1
How speech-like a track is. High values suggest spoken word; middle values can include mixed speech and music, including rap.
Liveness 0–1
Estimated presence of an audience. Higher values suggest a live performance; above 0.8 is a strong indicator.

Structure & harmony

Tempo BPM
Estimated overall pace of the track in beats per minute.
Duration ms → min
Track length in milliseconds; charts display it in minutes for readability.
Key −1, 0–11
Estimated pitch class: 0 is C, values continue by semitone through 11 (B); −1 means no key was detected.
Mode 0 / 1
Estimated modality: 0 is minor and 1 is major.

Catalog fields used for context

Popularity 0–100
Spotify’s current platform popularity score—not historical chart rank and not an audio feature.
Year & release date
When a track was released in the dataset; the charts group tracks by year.
Name, artists & ID
Track title, credited artist names, and the track’s dataset identifier.
Explicit 0 / 1
Whether the dataset flags the track as explicit content.

Source: Spotify Web API · Get Track’s Audio Features (documentation marks this endpoint deprecated).

The catalog is unevenly sampled across years. In this dataset, the 1920s contain no tracks above the survivor threshold; early-decade comparisons can also rest on very small groups.

THE SURVIVOR FILTER, IN SHORT

A current popularity score can point us toward songs that still circulate. Audio features can describe their sound. Neither can tell the whole story of memory, rediscovery, licensing, playlist placement, or the people who keep listening.
